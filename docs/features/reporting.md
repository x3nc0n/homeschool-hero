# Reporting

Homeschool Hero's reporting tools transform the day-to-day data you enter — grades, attendance, lesson completions — into the formal records homeschoolers need: report cards, transcripts, state compliance filings, and portfolio documentation.

---

## Grade Book

Go to **Assignments & Grading → Grade Book**.

The grade book is the live running record of academic performance. It updates every time a grade is approved.

### What you see

- **GPA** — calculated from all graded subjects using your family's grade scale
- **Subject summaries** — each course with its weighted average, percentage, and letter grade
- **Category breakdowns** — Homework, Quiz, and Test averages shown separately with their weights
- **Trend charts** — score history across the grading period
- **Individual assignments** — every graded item within a subject

### Filtering

| Filter | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Student** | Switch between students |
| **Subject** | Narrow to one course |
| **Grading period** | View a specific term, semester, or quarter |

Use grading period filtering at the end of a term to see exactly what will flow into the report card.

### Grade calculations

Grades flow through three layers:

1. **Assignment scores** — raw points earned / points possible
2. **Category averages** — weighted average of all assignments in each category (e.g., Test: 50%)
3. **Subject grade** — weighted combination of all category averages

Drop-lowest rules (if configured) remove the lowest N scores from a category before averaging.

### Recalculate

Select **Recalculate** to refresh grades after approving a batch of review-queue items. Useful after a high-volume grading session.

## Report Cards

Go to **Reports → Report Cards**.

Report cards are formal, finalized summaries of a student's performance for a grading period. Homeschool Hero generates them from live gradebook data and lets you add teacher commentary before exporting to PDF.

### What a report card includes

- **Student name and grading period**
- **Subject grades** — percentage, letter grade, and GPA points for each course
- **Cumulative GPA** for the period
- **Attendance summary** — days present, absent, tardy
- **Per-subject teacher comments**
- **General notes** from the primary teacher

### Generating a report card

1. Open **Reports → Report Cards**.
2. Select the student.
3. Select the grading period.
4. Click **Generate** — the system pulls current grades and attendance.
5. Review the draft. Verify all subjects are listed and scores look correct.
6. Add **teacher comments** to any subject that deserves elaboration.
7. Add **general notes** (overall progress, highlights, goals).
8. **Save the draft** — you can return and edit until you are ready.
9. When you are satisfied, click **Finalize**.
10. **Download PDF** for printing, emailing, or filing.

::: warning Finalizing is permanent
A finalized report card is locked. This preserves the integrity of the historical record. Make sure grades are correct and comments are complete before finalizing.
:::

### Drafts vs. finalized

| State | What you can do |
|---|---|
| **Draft** | Edit scores, add/edit comments, regenerate |
| **Finalized** | Download PDF, view — no further edits |

You can have multiple draft report cards in progress simultaneously (e.g., one per student, one per subject).

## Transcripts

Go to **Reports → Transcripts**.

Transcripts are a cumulative, multi-year academic record. They are especially important for high school students, dual-enrollment programs, and college applications.

### What a transcript includes

- **Student name** and family information
- **Course list** — every completed course across all school years
- **Credit hours** per course
- **Letter grade** per course
- **Honors or AP flag** where applicable
- **GPA** — term, annual, and cumulative
- **Teacher notes** per course
- **Overall academic summary**

### Generating a transcript

Transcripts are assembled from finalized report cards and gradebook data across all school years. To generate:

1. Open **Reports → Transcripts**.
2. Select the student.
3. Review the assembled course list.
4. Add or adjust credit values, honors flags, and notes as needed.
5. Export to PDF.

::: tip High school planning
For a student headed to college, start keeping transcripts current from 9th grade. It is much easier to build the transcript progressively than to reconstruct it at senior year.
:::

## Compliance Dashboard

Go to **Reports → Compliance**.

The compliance dashboard is a traffic-light overview of how well your homeschool records satisfy your state's requirements.

### Traffic-light status

| Color | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟢 **Green** | Requirement met |
| 🟡 **Yellow** | Warning — action may be needed soon |
| 🔴 **Red** | Non-compliant — action required |

### What the dashboard checks

- **Required subjects** — are all state-required courses being taught?
- **Instructional day count** — are you meeting the minimum school days?
- **Attendance records** — are attendance logs current?
- **Outstanding reports** — any required filings not yet submitted?
- **Missing assessments** — annual assessment results that are due?
- **Portfolio review status** — if your state requires a portfolio evaluation

### Switching school years

Use the year selector at the top to review compliance for any previous year. Useful when preparing for an audit, an annual assessment, or a notice-of-intent filing.

### Configuration

Compliance rules are driven by your family's **state code**, set in **Settings → Family**. The platform includes built-in rule templates for common state requirements, which your administrator can customize as needed.

## Compliance Reports

Go to **Reports → Compliance Reports**.

These are the formal documents some states require homeschoolers to file or maintain. Homeschool Hero generates them from your existing data and exports them as PDFs.

### Available report types

| Report type | Typical use |
|---|---|
| **Notice of intent** | File with your school district at the start of the year |
| **Annual assessment** | Record of end-of-year evaluation results |
| **Quarterly progress report** | Per-quarter summary for states that require it |
| **Attendance log** | Full day-by-day attendance record |
| **Portfolio review** | Evidence of learning for a portfolio evaluation |

Not all types are required in every state. Check your state's requirements and configure them in **Settings → Family → Compliance rules**.

### Generating a compliance report

1. Open **Reports → Compliance Reports**.
2. Select the **student**.
3. Select the **school year**.
4. Select the **report type**.
5. Select the **grading period** (if applicable).
6. Click **Generate** to create a draft.
7. Review the draft — the content is pulled from your attendance, lesson plans, and gradebook.
8. Add any required **notes** or explanations.
9. Click **Finalize** when the report is complete and accurate.
10. **Download PDF** to save, print, or submit.

::: warning
Finalized compliance reports are locked, just like report cards. Finalize only when the content is correct and ready to file.
:::

### Required checklist

The compliance reports page shows a checklist of which reports are required for the selected student and year. Use this as your to-do list when preparing end-of-year documentation.

## Portfolio and Learning Journal

Go to **Reports → Portfolio**.

The portfolio is a curated collection of learning evidence. It is useful for portfolio-review states, co-op documentation, annual evaluations, and as a meaningful record of a student's growth over time.

### Entry types

| Type | What it captures |
|---|---|
| **Work sample** | A finished piece of student work |
| **Journal** | A written reflection — parent or student authored |
| **Milestone** | A skill mastered or goal reached |
| **Photo** | A project, experiment, field trip, or creative work |
| **Note** | An observation or brief record |

### Adding an entry

1. Open **Reports → Portfolio**.
2. Select the student.
3. Click **Add entry**.
4. Choose the entry type.
5. Enter the title and date.
6. Add a description or body text.
7. Optionally attach files (photos, PDFs, scans).
8. Optionally link to a subject or assignment.
9. Add tags for easy filtering later.
10. Save.

### Linking to assignments and subjects

Linking a portfolio entry to an assignment connects the evidence to the gradebook record. This is especially useful when you want to show a portfolio evaluator not just that the work was done, but that it was graded.

### Collections

Collections let you group entries into a thematic set.

Examples:
- "Science portfolio — 2025–2026"
- "Reading growth — Q1 through Q4"
- "Best work — year end presentation"
- "State portfolio review materials"

### Sharing a collection

1. Open a collection.
2. Toggle **Make public**.
3. Copy the generated share link.

The share link gives view-only access to that collection — no login required. Share it with grandparents, evaluators, co-op coordinators, or for a portfolio review submission.

### Learning journal

Within the portfolio, **Journal** entries form a running learning journal. Encourage students (or write observations yourself) after significant lessons, field trips, or project completions. Over a school year, the journal becomes a rich narrative record of what was learned and how.

## Reporting Workflow: End-of-Term Checklist

At the end of each grading period:

1. **Close out grading** — make sure all assignments have been uploaded, reviewed, and finalized in the Review Queue.
2. **Update attendance** — fill in any missing days or pending excuses.
3. **Check the Compliance dashboard** — resolve any yellow or red items.
4. **Generate report cards** — one per student per grading period.
5. **Review and comment** — add teacher observations while the term is fresh.
6. **Finalize and download** — archive the PDF.
7. **Generate compliance reports** (if required) — attendance log, quarterly report, etc.
8. **Update portfolio** — add highlights, milestones, and notable work from the term.
9. **Export data** — run a full family export from **Data → Export** for off-site archiving.
10. **Run a backup** — confirm the last backup succeeded from **Settings → Backups**.

Following this checklist every term means end-of-year reporting takes minutes, not days.
